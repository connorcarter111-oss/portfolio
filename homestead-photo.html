Homestead · Photo Direction

Directing all photo and video production for Homestead Coffee — every product shot, every space image, every piece of content that built an audience of 8,000+ from nothing.

The Problem

A brand with no visual content is invisible. Homestead had great coffee, a beautiful space, and zero presence online. The challenge was building a content engine from scratch — one that felt authentic to the brand, not like a social media strategy.

Great coffee deserves great photography. From the first day Homestead opened its doors, the visual content wasn't an afterthought — it was a core part of how the brand showed up in the world.


I directed all photo and video production for the brand — shooting campaigns, product details, space atmospherics, and social content. No paid ads, no boosted posts. Just consistent, considered creative work that gave people a reason to follow, share, and show up.


Key Decisions

  • Shot entirely on location, never in a controlled studio — because Homestead's identity is inseparable from its physical spaces, and that had to show in every image.
  • Chose natural light and imperfect detail over controlled, polished studio aesthetics — latte art drips, hands in frame, morning shadows. The images needed to feel like something you'd want to be inside of, not an advertisement for coffee.
  • Built a seasonal content cadence that gave the audience a reason to keep coming back — content that changed with the menu, the light, and the time of year rather than repeating the same product shots on a loop.

01 · Product Photography

Making the ordinary feel worth stopping for.

A latte is a latte until the right light hits it. Product photography for Homestead was about finding the shot that made someone stop scrolling — the pour at the right moment, the steam curling against a muted background, the espresso pulling into cream.


Every product image was shot to work at multiple scales: thumbnail on a phone screen, hero on the website, print in a wholesale catalog. Consistency was the goal — not sameness, but a coherent visual voice.

Seasonal menus got seasonal campaigns. When we launched the peach cold brew, it got a full shoot — fresh peaches, warm afternoon light, the kind of image that sold the drink before anyone tasted it.


The matcha, the pourover, the iced pour — each one was treated as a product launch, not just a menu addition. That's the difference between documentation and creative direction.

02 · Seasonal Campaigns

Every menu drop treated as a launch.

Seasonal offerings are an opportunity to create a moment — something people anticipate and share. The peach cold brew, the matcha latte, the pour series — each one got a distinct visual treatment that felt new while still being unmistakably Homestead.

03 · Food & Table

The full table, not just the cup.

Coffee is one part of a morning ritual. The croissant next to it, the plate it's served on, the table it rests on — all of that is part of the experience. Food and table content rounded out the brand story, giving followers a reason to linger in the feed the same way they linger in the café.

Content Strategy

8,000 followers. Zero ad spend.

The content strategy was simple in principle and disciplined in execution: post things worth looking at, consistently, with a clear point of view. No gimmicks, no trends for trend's sake. Just images that made people feel something about coffee and the place that made it.


I built a content calendar around the rhythm of the café — seasonal drops, behind-the-scenes roastery content, quiet morning shots, product launches. Every post fit a role: some grew reach, some drove traffic, some just kept the community warm.
